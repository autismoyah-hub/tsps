# HD authoring techniques for revision 242

The closest controlled example is the Sample table, object 23526. Both revisions have four parts, 1,187 vertices, 2,126 faces, 34 colour words and an alpha value of 25. In HD, 2,074 faces use 14 texture IDs. In 239, zero faces use a texture. An author can reproduce that change in surface detail while keeping the existing triangle budget. [Scenery sheet](sheets/object-1.png)

| Technique | Measured HD example and 239 control | Can we rebuild it in 242? |
|---|---|---|
| Concentrate geometry where the silhouette changes | Shadow-Forger Ihlakhizan 10156 has 2,920 faces and 1,553 vertices. Sarachnis 8713 has 1,346 and 686. Conversely, Har'Lakk 9911 uses 2,108 faces against K'ril 3129 at 2,754. Mesh density varies by creature. | **Yes.** Both examples fit ordinary indexed triangles. Design and light a new silhouette before spending faces on small surface details. |
| Use multiple materials on one continuous mesh | Avatar of Destruction 8596 has 2,816 textured faces out of 2,831, using 12 texture IDs. Its same-name 239 counterpart 10521 has 2,830 faces with zero textured. Both are one part with 32-frame stand and walk cycles. | **With textures-alpha support.** Allocate authored target materials and use simple mapping bases. Numeric HD texture IDs address a different catalogue. |
| Texture equipment independently of topology | Diving apparatus item 7535 has 176 vertices, 303 faces and 11 HSL words in both caches. HD assigns all 303 faces across five texture IDs. OSRS assigns none. | **With textures-alpha support.** New small tileable materials can carry cloth, leather and metal detail on existing face groups. |
| Layer texture detail onto translucent geometry | Lamp stall 22772 keeps three parts, 1,660 faces and alpha bytes 144, 160, 176, 192 and 208 in both caches. HD additionally textures 1,649 faces across 13 materials. | **With textures-alpha support.** Keep face alpha, priority and transparent texels as independent authoring decisions. Several levels of face transparency already work in classic. |
| Separate material boundaries from part boundaries | Yk'Lagor 11886 carries ten textures on one 3,294-face part. Its large demon control K'ril 3129 assembles five parts and uses 27 colour words on 2,754 faces. Bal'lak 10141 and General Graardor 2215 both use two parts. | **Yes** for part assembly and face colours. **With textures-alpha support** for multiple materials. Separate a piece when it has a rigging, reuse or placement reason. |
| Spend frames selectively on a characteristic movement | Icy Bones 10057 has a 64-frame walk, versus 19 for Ice giant 7878. Flesh-Spoiler 11939 has a 120-frame stand and 40-frame walk, versus 20 and 20 in the Abyssal Sire control 5886. Yk'Lagor alternates 32- and 66-frame idles but walks in 16 frames, the same frame count as K'ril. | **Yes** for authored classic sequences with compatible bases and labels. Picking a weighted idle at runtime also requires a caller that selects the sequence. Counts measure frames, not elapsed seconds. |
| Construct a spot effect from overlapping alpha bands | HD spot 2768 uses 798 faces, 61 colour words, 16 distinct nonzero alpha values and a 20-frame sequence. Its size/duration control, OSRS spot 2955, uses 860 faces, four colours, four nonzero alpha values and 20 frames. | **With textures-alpha support** to author/preserve the bands. The standalone spot and frame sequence fit 242. Palette and silhouette geometry can be rebuilt directly. |
| Generate detail procedurally and project it across a surface | Nomad model 51518 uses 25 box, two cylindrical and one spherical mapping records. At the definition level, 1,693 of its 2,282 faces are textured. OSRS Nomad 10516 has 1,708 faces and zero textured. | **With textures-alpha support** for a baked appearance using fresh pixels and planar bases. **Needs a client change** to interpret the HD graph and projected mapping records natively on the Rust classic path. |
| Attach effects to a specific animated face or vertex | Yk'Lagor model 59609 has two type-33 billboards bound to faces 3293 and 3292. Flesh-Spoiler model 55399 binds three emitters, types 152, 150 and 151, to faces 1, 0 and 2. Revision 614 contains 126 emitter-bearing models and 59 billboard-bearing models. | **Needs a client change** for these embedded bindings and their runtime behavior. An independently authored animated spot mesh can approximate a particular pose or event using 242 facilities. |
| Keep finer-than-classic vertex positions | Model 25 expands from a 108 × 150 × 194 box in 601 to 432 × 600 × 776 stored subunits in 614. The classic conversion shifts each coordinate two bits. | **Yes** when positions fall on the whole-unit grid. **Needs a client change** to carry and draw arbitrary subunit positions in the model format at the same world scale. |

The comparison covers selected high-combat or large creatures, inventory models with textures, and same-ID/name scenery with both textured and translucent faces. It is a study of examples, not a population estimate. Same-name Avatar, equipment and scenery controls are the best basis for attributing a change to materials. The other bosses are selected by body type and encounter role. Spot controls minimize a distance in face count, sequence length and alpha-face share. Their encounter roles may differ. [All measurements and paired images](STUDY.md)

The cache directly connects the two billboard and three emitter examples to their models. Boss-to-standalone-spot attribution has a weaker basis. None of the ten selected bosses' measured idle/walk sequences shared a frame group or frame base with a sequenced spot in the 614 scan. The spot sheet gives model-ID and sequence-ID distances so the reader can assess the proposed batches. A distance of one model but 3,807 sequence IDs for Icy Bones is especially weak evidence. Original encounter logic or a recorded encounter would be needed to establish which effects actually fired. [Spot candidate data](spot-study.json)
