# Extracting a working method from past work, following the owner's verdicts

## When to use it

There is a body of earlier work of mixed quality, its own records call most of it successful, and the owner knows which of it was actually good. A new team should start from what worked. The job is to find that work, write down how it was done concretely enough to repeat, and set it beside the work that failed.

Use this where acceptance is a person's judgment. Agents' own labels in such records are unreliable in both directions: here the best work was stamped unapproved throughout, and the failed work carried a reviewer's pass.

## The brief

```
Find the best earlier work on <what>, work out exactly how it was done, and bring the material a new
team needs onto this machine. The owner has told me which work was good and which was not. That is the
most valuable information we have, so this job follows the owner's judgment, not the agents' own.

What the owner said (verbatim where quoted): <each statement, quoted, with what it refers to>.

Read first: <the earlier survey, and a warning about anything in it now known to be wrong>.
<Access and its rules: change nothing, no secrets, do not read private conversation records wholesale;
you may search them for the work you are tracing and read the relevant passages.>

Part 1: identify the work the owner means. Its names or numbers, dates, where its files are: the briefs
given to the agents, the skills and role files in force then, scripts, outputs, review notes, and any
record of the owner's reaction. If more than one candidate fits, describe each and say which the
evidence points to and why. Establish which harness, model and settings produced it.

Part 2: extract the method, concretely enough to repeat. The actual procedure, step by step: inputs,
tools, what the agent was told to do and not to do, the conventions that made the result good, how it
was presented for review, who reviewed and by what rules. Quote the key instructions. Then set it
beside the failed work: what differed in brief, method, model, settings, inputs and review. Be specific
about what the failed run ignored.

Part 3: build a calibration set from the owner's own verdicts. Every item for which a reaction from the
owner is on record, from any of the work, with the reaction quoted and dated and the artefact it refers
to. Include only cases where the owner's own words or decision are on record. This becomes the standard
a reviewer agent is tested against, so precision matters more than quantity.

Part 4: copy what a new team needs to <path>, under <size>, in priority order: <list>. Leave out
regenerable bulk and anything that looks like a credential. Verify each transfer and write a manifest.

Part 5: write the findings to <file>, under <size>: which work was best and the evidence; the method
step by step; what the failed work did differently; the calibration set as a table; the tools and their
state; what a new machine needs to repeat it, including anything that needed special hardware or a paid
service and what it cost; what could not be determined.

Reply with: which work it was and how sure you are; the method in ten plain lines; the most important
differences in the failed work; the harness, model and settings each used; how many owner verdicts you
found; what was copied; and the questions only the owner can answer.
```

## What a good result looked like

The best work identified through the owner's own words on three dates, not through its labels. A twelve-step method with the key instructions quoted. A side-by-side table showing that the failed run differed in four ways at once, including a lower reasoning-effort setting nobody had mentioned. Twenty-three owner verdicts with their artefacts, and one clean three-step case for testing a reviewer. One correction to the owner's own recollection, stated plainly.

## What to guard against

- **Blaming the obvious variable.** The owner attributed the failure to the model. The record showed model, harness, effort setting and brief had all changed. Ask for every difference, and do not conclude which one mattered.
- **Paths mangled in the write-up.** Backslashes in a table were interpreted as escapes. Have the agent keep an authoritative path list in a plain file and point to it.
- **Sources that move.** See [survey-machine.md](survey-machine.md).
- **Private material.** Verdicts and artefacts stay on the machine; the method document may go in a private repository; images do not.
